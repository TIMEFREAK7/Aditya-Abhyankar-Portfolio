Case Study

Defending a Delay Claim

Two contractors claimed they had been given their work fronts late. The schedules had not been updated for 18 months, so I built the defence from the project correspondence.

Context

I worked as the planning engineer for the project management consultant (PMC) on a large greenfield manufacturing plant in India. Two of the main contractors, one for the civil and structural works and one for the electrical, HVAC, fire and MEP works, raised claims that they had been given their work fronts later than had been agreed.

What was at stake

If the contractors could prove their claims, the client would have had to pay them a large amount in compensation.

My part

  • Compared the contractors' master schedules, the client's master schedule and our own, checking actual start and finish dates against the baselines across 960+ activities.
  • Compiled the emails showing where the delay was the contractor's own, in a log of 90+ items that set each commitment against the actual date.
  • Built the registers, briefs and management presentations used to contest the claims.
  • Colleagues gathered and passed me the emails; the analysis and the case were mine.

The key decision

None of the master schedules had been updated for the previous 18 months, so for that period the schedules could not show who had caused the delays. I based the defence for that period on the emails instead: what each party had committed to, and what actually happened.

Outcome

We showed that the delays had been caused by the contractors themselves. The delays on our side were minimal, and the claims were settled for a fraction of the amount claimed.

What I would do differently

  • Keep the schedules updated from the day I joined, so the record exists before anyone needs it.
  • Involve more people in gathering the data.
  • Run a brainstorming session at the start to agree a better plan for building the case.

Client and contractor names and claim values are left out for confidentiality.
